    Tips for iPhone
    • Allow the microphone once. In Safari, tap aA › Website Settings › Microphone › Allow, so you aren't asked at every restart.
    • Dictation must be on. Settings › General › Keyboard › Enable Dictation. Speech is processed by Apple, the same way iPhone dictation works.
    • Keep Verbatim on screen. iOS pauses listening when you switch apps or lock the phone. Verbatim notes the gap and resumes when you come back.
    • Long lectures: plug in, and turn off Low Power Mode, which can let the screen lock.
    • Place the phone near whoever talks most, screen up, with nothing covering the bottom microphone.
    • If recording won't start from the Home Screen icon, open Verbatim in Safari instead. The Home Screen app keeps its own separate history.

    Privacy, security and FAQ

    Verbatim has no server, no account, no analytics and no tracking. This page explains where your data goes, what can go wrong, and your responsibilities when you transcribe other people.

    Privacy and your data

    Where do my recordings go?

    Only to these places:

    • Your voice goes to Apple’s speech recognition, the same service iPhone dictation uses. Apple may process it on its servers.
    • The transcript, notes and settings are saved only on this iPhone, in Safari’s storage for this website.
    • Summaries: when you ask for one, the transcript goes from your iPhone straight to Anthropic, using your own API key.
    Is audio recorded or kept?

    Verbatim never saves audio, only text. If you allow it, Apple may keep samples of dictation audio to improve its service. To turn that off, open the Settings app › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements and switch off Improve Siri & Dictation. See Apple’s privacy policy.

    Can the developer, Anthropic or GitHub see my transcripts?

    The developer and GitHub can’t. The website only delivers the app’s code, and your transcripts stay on your iPhone.

    The one exception is a summary request, which goes to Anthropic. Under Anthropic’s commercial terms, API data isn’t used to train models by default and is kept only for a limited time. See Anthropic’s privacy policy. If you paste the prompt into the Claude app instead, your Claude account’s privacy settings apply.

    How is my API key stored?

    It’s stored in Safari’s storage on this iPhone, without extra encryption, and sent only to Anthropic. The app’s security policy blocks connections to any other server. Use a key just for Verbatim, and set a monthly spending limit for it in the Anthropic Console.

    What does a summary cost?

    With an API key, you pay Anthropic directly. A one-hour meeting costs roughly 10–20¢. Without a key, copy the prompt into the Claude app, which uses your Claude plan instead.

    How do I delete my data?
    • One recording: open it, tap ⋯, then Delete recording.
    • The API key: in Verbatim’s Settings, leave the key field empty and tap Save.
    • Everything: open the Settings app › Apps › Safari › Advanced › Website Data (on iOS 17, Settings › Safari › Advanced). Find rogue030.github.io and delete it.

    Security and risks

    Can I lose my transcripts?

    Yes. They exist only on this iPhone, so they’re lost if:

    • Safari erases the website’s data because you haven’t opened Verbatim for about a week of browsing. Opening Verbatim regularly prevents this.
    • You clear Safari’s history and website data.
    • Your iPhone is lost, replaced or reset.

    Back up regularly: Verbatim Settings › Back up all, then save the file to Files or iCloud Drive. Restore it from the same screen.

    What if I lose my iPhone?

    Your iPhone’s passcode and built-in encryption protect your transcripts. If you saved an API key, revoke it in the Anthropic Console so nobody else can use it.

    Does anything else share Verbatim’s storage?

    Browsers give every site at the same address one shared storage area. Verbatim runs at rogue030.github.io, so another web app published at that address could read Verbatim’s data. Verbatim is currently the only app published there.

    Who controls the app’s code?

    Your iPhone loads Verbatim’s code from the GitHub account rogue030, and updates arrive automatically. Whoever controls that account can change the app. Verbatim’s security policy limits the damage a bad change could do: the page may only run its own code and may only connect to Anthropic.

    Is it safe on public Wi‑Fi?

    Yes. Verbatim loads over HTTPS, and summary requests to Anthropic are encrypted too.

    Recording and the law

    This is general information, not legal advice. The rules depend on where you are.

    Do I need permission to transcribe a conversation?

    Often, yes. In many places a saved transcript counts as recording a conversation. Some US states, including California, Florida, Illinois, Pennsylvania and Washington, require everyone’s consent, and so do many countries. The simplest habit: say “I’m transcribing this” at the start, and stop if anyone objects.

    Can I transcribe lectures?

    Check your school’s policy first. Many allow it for personal study and for accessibility needs. The lecturer usually owns the lecture content, so don’t share or publish transcripts without permission.

    Can I use it for work meetings?

    Check your employer’s rules on confidential information and AI tools before you summarize work meetings with Claude. In the EU and UK, a transcript is personal data of everyone who speaks in it, under the GDPR.

    How accurate is it?

    Accuracy depends on distance from the phone, background noise and accents. Names, numbers and technical terms are often wrong, and AI summaries can contain mistakes. Check anything important against the original. Tap any line to fix it.

    Using Verbatim

    Why does recording stop when I lock the screen or switch apps?

    iPhone only lets Safari use the microphone while the page is on screen. Verbatim keeps the screen awake while recording, notes any gap in the transcript, and resumes when you come back.

    Why can’t I record from the Home Screen icon?

    On many iOS versions, Apple doesn’t offer speech recognition to web apps opened from the Home Screen. Open Verbatim in Safari to record. The Home Screen version keeps its own separate history.

    Why does Safari keep asking for the microphone?

    In Safari, tap aA in the address bar › Website Settings › Microphone › Allow.
